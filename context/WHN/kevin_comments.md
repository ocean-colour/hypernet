Cluster 5 in the Pooled spectral shape cluster is obviously not good. We need to see how that passed QC.
 

I note that you seem most interested in reflectance spectra that are colocated with other measurements, e.g. IOPs. I understand the use case. HYPERNETS is a single parameter network (reflectance) but is also designed as a core for expansion to other measurands according to the resources and interests of the site PI. I strongly believe that water reflectance is the most important parameter to measure. I would, of course, like to have additional measurands at all sites, following a common protocol, but this is logistically and financially not possible. Some of the PIs do have extra measurements, usually from short duration deployments of extra instruments. I will ask the PIs what they have and suggest that HYPERNETS advertises this to the data users.

 

While you may be disappointed at reflectance measurements without extra measurands, I note that there are important applications for a reflectance-only dataset. Firstly satellite validation, of course. Secondly water quality monitoring for local users, e.g. time series of chlorophyll a and phytoplankton type for water quality managers and aquatic biologists. Thirdly, aquatic optics researchers investigating spectral variability itself. Fourthly, a few sites are colocated with AERONET-OC measurements so the comparison of data from these two systems provides information on data quality and ideas for improvement of processing.

 

Personally, I would most like to see HYPERNETS sites supplemented by automated flow cytometers (phytoplankton species composition), turbidimeters and/or (chlorophyll) fluorimeters. That would add powerful datasets for validation of in-water parameter retrieval algorithms. IOPs are more difficult to measure and usually an intermediate, explanatory parameter, not an end-user parameter.
